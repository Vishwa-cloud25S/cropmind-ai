# 03 — Market Research

**Prepared:** 2026-09-28 · **Rule:** every figure has a source and a date; where sources disagree we show the range, not the favourite.

## 1. Demand driver: losses are large and measured

- **Up to 40% of global crop production is lost to pests annually**; plant diseases cost
  the global economy **over $220 billion per year**; invasive insects at least $70 billion
  ([FAO newsroom, 2021-06-02](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/Climate-change-fans-spread-of-pests-and-threatens-plants-and-crops-new-FAO-study/en), accessed 2026-09-28).
- The UK government's Food Security Report cites the FAO estimate of **20–40%** of global
  crop production lost to plant diseases and pests
  ([GOV.UK, UKFSR 2021](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2021/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2021-theme-1-global-food-availability), accessed 2026-09-28).
- Climate change is expanding pest ranges and pressure (same FAO review) — the problem is
  growing, not shrinking.

## 5. Adoption barriers we must design for (sourced)

- UK agri-tech adoption faces cost barriers, skills gaps and knowledge-transfer problems;
  startups struggle to scale beyond pilots and the investment climate is risk-averse
  ([Ei Operator, 2025](https://www.eioperator.com/the-transition-to-automation-in-uk-crop-growing/), accessed 2026-09-28).
- Consequences baked into the plan: software-only (no hardware capital), free tier as
  risk-free trial, agronomists as trust-multipliers, honest performance numbers to avoid
  over-promise churn.

## 6. What we deliberately did NOT claim

- No "AI will replace agronomists" — the product requires human review by design.
- No yield-uplift percentage — we have not measured one; pilots must produce that number honestly.
- No India TAM detour — India is field-validation context; the business is UK-first (visa alignment, see 05).
